The problem Laminary names

Five people. Five AIs. No shared memory.

When every developer picks their own AI tool, each chat becomes an isolated silo. Laminary's homepage lists the symptoms:

  • Two AIs build the same thing twice because neither knows about the other's work.
  • A chat hits its context limit and the work is stuck with nobody able to pick it up.
  • Nobody knows who is working on what across the team.

The problem is real. The question is what the fix should look like: a bridge between silos, or one workspace with no silos.

Source: https://laminary.dev/
The competitor

What Laminary actually is

  • An MCP connector: paste one address (app.laminary.dev/mcp) into each AI tool. Nothing to install.
  • A control room: a browser tab showing every teammate's AI session on a shared timeline.
  • Note-based handoffs: one AI leaves notes another AI's session can pick up.
  • A weekly receipt: tokens the team did not spend twice.
  • Privacy levels per session: goal only, summaries, or full chat. Keys filtered on your machine.
  • Three checks on notes: cleaned, labeled as a teammate's note, and held if it looks like an attack.
  • EU storage, no code, no chats unless shared.
  • Free beta now, team pricing after.
Source: https://laminary.dev/ · https://laminary.dev/guide/

Shared memory: Threads

Amp's core primitive already is shared team memory. Threads are saved, shareable records of every agent session.

  • Share with the workspace or groups: teammates open any thread and see the full history, not a summary.
  • Continue from another thread: point a new thread at an existing one's context and pick up exactly where it stopped.
  • Searchable: every past session is findable, so done work is visible before anyone starts it again.
This replaces Laminary's "goal, status, files touched, outputs, handoff" wall with the full primary record, not a digest of it.
Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/threads · markdown: https://ampcode.com/docs/markdown/threads

No clashes: Multiplayer

Laminary "catches" a clash after two AIs already started the same work. Amp prevents the clash by making the work happen in one place.

  • Multiple people prompt the same thread at once, on orbs and shared runners.
  • Mention a teammate's agent into an existing thread and it joins with full history.
  • Notifications keep everyone current without a separate control room.
Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/collaborate/multiplayer
Amp's answer · 3 of 6

The control room: Spaces

Laminary's browser-tab timeline shows what every teammate's AI is doing. A Space is that view, except it also runs the agents.

  • One Space groups all of a project's threads, so what's done, in progress and stopped is visible at a glance.
  • Shared with the workspace, groups, or specific people — never with link-holders.
  • @Amp from Slack routes requests into the Space, which starts the threads.
Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/spaces

"Who's on what": Workspaces

Laminary answers "nobody knows who's on what" with a project wall. Amp answers it with org-level structure.

  • Workspace-wide thread sharing with directory-synced identity in enterprise plans.
  • Groups scope visibility to a team, project or function.
  • Audited access: opening a shared thread from outside the workspace re-confirms identity and shows an audit notice.
Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/collaborate/workspaces
Amp's answer · 5 of 6

Teammate notes: Artifacts

Laminary passes cleaned, labeled, attack-screened notes between AIs. Amp's artifacts are richer shared documents that live with the threads that produced them.

  • Editable and commentable by everyone who can open the thread, including viewers.
  • Combine findings from several threads into one reviewable report, plan or dashboard.
  • Documents, visuals and files with revision tracking and access control per artifact.
Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/threads#artifacts · https://ampcode.com/docs/threads#share-an-artifact
Amp's answer · 6 of 6

Cross-agent coordination: Puck

Laminary's pitch is that one AI benefits from another AI's notes. Puck coordinates agents directly, inside the workspace.

  • Puck launches and coordinates agents for you across threads and environments.
  • Agents hand work to other agents with full context, not summaries that passed through a third party.
  • The trust boundary stays yours: coordination happens inside your workspace, with no external processor reading session digests.
Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/puck

The trade nobody prices

A third party now reads your session digests

Laminary's model

Session summaries flow through Laminary's EU-hosted service. Their FAQ says no code and no chats unless shared, and notes are screened for injection. It is still a new trust boundary and a new MCP endpoint in every tool.

https://laminary.dev/privacy/

Amp's model

The shared record lives in your workspace. Access is workspace-scoped, group-scoped or audited. No external processor receives digests of your sessions, so the injection and leakage surface does not grow.

https://ampcode.com/docs/collaborate/workspaces

Neither is wrong. But if a team is already inside one workspace, adding a connector is added risk for coordination it already has.

Segmenting the market

Who actually needs Laminary?

Needs it
  • Mixed-tool startups (2 to 6 devs) where Cursor, Claude Code, ChatGPT and Codex all run at once.
  • Agencies and contractors handing work across different tools through copy-paste.
  • ChatGPT-only teams with no agentic tool and no shared project memory at all.
Doesn't need it
  • Amp teams: threads, Spaces, artifacts, multiplayer and Puck already provide shared memory, handoffs and clash avoidance.
  • Single-tool enterprise teams whose vendor ships shared context natively.
The market is fragmentation, not coordination. Coordination only becomes a product when the tools themselves are silos. Laminary bets multi-tool fragmentation stays the norm; Amp bets teams converge on one agentic workspace.
Bottom line

Verdict

  • Necessary with Amp? No. Every capability Laminary sells maps to an Amp-native feature, listed slide by slide above with doc links.
  • Useful as a bridge? Possibly, if your team really splits across Amp, Cursor, ChatGPT and Claude Code. That mixed-tool gap is the one thing Amp does not cover.
  • Cost of testing: the beta is free, so the bridge case is cheap to try. For an Amp-only workflow, skip it: same features, no extra trust boundary.
