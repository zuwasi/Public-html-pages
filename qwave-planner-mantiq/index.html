Q-Wave Planner · public review copy

A post-quantum migration planner that is simulated, proven, and executable

Q-Wave Planner decides which systems a bank should migrate to post-quantum cryptography first, and it proves the answer is optimal before a single engineer-week is spent. It is a working demonstration of the MantiQ workflow: a Wolfram Quantum Framework simulation, a Lean 4 certificate, and an executable planner that all compute the same numbers.

What this isOne instance, four layers, one answer

A fictional bank (Meridian Bank) faces the question Q.security helps customers answer: with a fixed budget of engineer-weeks, which cryptographic assets do we migrate to post-quantum first? Six candidate migrations, each with a cost in weeks and a risk score in points, one dependency (the portal needs the gateway), and a budget of 12 or 16 engineer-weeks.

The same instance runs through the whole MantiQ stack: the exposure model comes from a real peer-reviewed paper (Gupta & Mittal 2026, Cybersecurity 9:147), a Python module integrates it exactly, Wolfram's Quantum Framework builds and simulates the QAOA circuit for it, Lean 4 proves the optimum is optimal (by exhaustive decision over all 64 plans), and the browser app recomputes everything live. The demo's conclusion: at 12 engineer-weeks the best first wave is Payments + VPN, worth 70 points; at 16 weeks four extra weeks flip the whole wave to Payments + Gateway + Portal, worth 92. A naive exposure-ranked list misses that flip.

What it can do for youSix things you get in the next five minutes

See the flaw in ranked lists

Open the live planner and move the budget slider: the optimal plan changes at 15 weeks in a way no exposure-ranked list predicts. You get the intuition in one drag.

Get a certificate, not a claim

The optimum facts are Lean 4 theorems, proved by exhaustive decision over all 64 plans: 3006 build jobs, zero errors, zero sorries. You get machine-checked optimality, not a vendor assertion.

Run the quantum layer yourself

A QAOA statevector computed live in your browser at Wolfram's search-found angles, matching Wolfram's stored distribution to TVD 5.9e-16. You get the same circuit in two independent implementations.

Show the cost of waiting

The paper's exposure curve, drawn live: starting the migration in 2026 leaves E[f] = 0.366 of a 10-year asset's exposure window open; waiting until 2030 raises it to 0.539. You get the "why now" argument with numbers behind it.

Reproduce everything locally

Every number regenerates from source on your machine: Python for the exposure model and enumeration, Wolfram for QAOA and charts, Lean for the certificate. No account, no cloud, no trust required.

A template for your own instance

Swap in your own asset inventory, costs and scores, and the same four layers re-plan your migration. At this size (six projects) enumeration is exact; past roughly 30 projects the same cost layer hands over to integer programming.

py -3.12 classiq/generate_qmod.py  # the Classiq QMOD artifact
py -3 app/build_app.py             # rebuild the executable planner

Requirements: Python 3, Wolfram Language (free Wolfram Engine works), Lean 4 + Mathlib (lake), the Classiq Python SDK on Python 3.12 (only for the QMOD artifact), Node.js with Playwright only if you want to re-record the demo video.

Honest scopeWhat is proven, what is measured, what is assumed

  • Proven (Lean 4): at 12 engineer-weeks the optimum is {PAY, VPN} = 70, it is unique, and every feasible plan is worth at most 70; at 16 weeks the optimum is {PAY, GWY, PRT} = 92. The flip theorem covers the 12-to-16-week comparison, not every budget.
  • Measured (Python + Wolfram, cross-checked): the E[f] exposure model, the 64-plan enumeration at every budget, the QAOA distributions, and the 4096-shot sample statistics. The two pipelines recompute each other's arithmetic.
  • Best found, not certified global: the QAOA angles (dense-grid multi-start search). At budget 16 with p=2 the best found P(optimal) is 0.0075; the enumeration layer, not the sampler, makes the decision.
  • Assumed (disclosed in data/instance.json): the 0-25 business-impact bonus weighting. Without it the 12-week optimum is unique ({PAY, VPN} = 37) and the 16-week optimum is a three-way tie at 44, so the flip is a consequence of that assumption. The paper's Tables 8 and 9 are not consistent with its own Eq (2), so those values are recomputed from Eq (2).
  • Fictional: Meridian Bank and its assets are invented for the demo. Not affiliated with Q.security.
