The problem 02

Five people. Five AIs. No shared memory.

The problem Laminary names on its homepage is real, and it is worth restating precisely

Duplicate work

Two AIs build the same thing

Neither session knows the other exists, so both implement the feature and the team merges one and throws the other away.

Stuck sessions

A chat hits its limit

The context window fills, the session dies, and nobody else can pick the work up because the state lived in one person's chat history.

No visibility

Nobody knows who is on what

Five isolated chat histories mean the only project board is what people remember to tell each other.

The question is not whether the problem exists. The question is what the fix looks like: a bridge between five silos, or one workspace with no silos at all.
Source: https://laminary.dev/
The competitor 03

What Laminary actually is

A connector and a browser control room between AI tools your team already uses

The product
  • MCP connector: paste one address, app.laminary.dev/mcp, into each AI tool. Nothing to install.
  • Control room: a browser tab showing every teammate's AI session on a shared timeline.
  • Note-based handoffs: one AI leaves notes that another AI's session picks up.
  • Weekly receipt: tokens the team did not spend twice, with a per-task ledger.
The posture
  • Privacy levels per session: goal only, summaries, or full chat. Keys and secrets filtered on your machine first.
  • Three checks on every note: cleaned of hidden text and keys, labeled as a teammate's note, held if it looks like an attack.
  • EU storage: no code, no chats unless a session turns on full sharing.
  • Free beta now, founding team pricing after.

Amp's answer 1 of 6 04

Shared memory: Threads

Amp's core primitive already is shared team memory, and it is the primary record, not a digest

Share

Workspace and group visibility

Threads are shared with the workspace or with groups, so teammates open any thread and see the full history, not a summary of it.

Pick up

Continue from another thread

Point a new thread at an existing one's context and it continues exactly where the last one stopped, with the same evidence.

Search

Every session is findable

Done work is visible before anyone starts it again. The receipt Laminary sells is the side effect of threads being one shared record.

This replaces Laminary's goal, status, files touched, outputs and handoff wall with the full primary record.

Amp's answer 2 of 6 05

No clashes: Multiplayer

Laminary catches a clash after two AIs already started the same work. Amp prevents the clash structurally.

One session

Multiple people, one thread

Teammates prompt the same thread at once, on orbs and on shared runners, so there is nothing to double up.

Handoff

Mention a teammate's agent

A mention brings another person's agent into the existing thread with full history, not a copy-paste summary.

Awareness

Notifications built in

Mention-driven notifications keep everyone current without a separate control-room tab.

Amp's answer 3 of 6 06

The control room: Spaces

Laminary's browser tab shows what every teammate's AI is doing. A Space is that view, and it also runs the agents.

One place

All of a project's threads

Done, in progress, stopped: the Space groups the project's threads so the state is visible at a glance.

Scoped

Shared with people, not links

A Space is shared with the workspace, with groups, or with single people, never with anyone holding a link.

Routed

@Amp from Slack

A mention in a linked Slack channel routes the request into the Space, which starts the threads.

Docs: https://ampcode.com/docs/spaces

Who is on what: Workspaces

Laminary answers the visibility gap with a project wall. Amp answers it with org-level structure.

Identity

Workspace-wide sharing

Threads are visible across the workspace, with directory-synced identity in enterprise plans.

Scoping

Groups

Groups scope visibility to a team, project or function, so a thread reaches exactly the people it should.

Audit

Audited external access

Amp's answer 5 of 6 08

Teammate notes: Artifacts

Laminary passes cleaned, labeled, attack-screened notes between AIs. Artifacts are richer documents that live with the threads that produced them.

Editable

Everyone who can open the thread

Artifacts are editable and commentable by everyone with thread access, including viewers.

Combinable

Findings from several threads

One artifact combines the work of several threads into a single reviewable report, plan or dashboard.

Controlled

Documents, visuals, files

Revision tracking and per-artifact access control, from thread-only to public link, set by the owner.

Amp's answer 6 of 6 09

Cross-agent coordination: Puck

Laminary's pitch is that one AI benefits from another AI's notes. Puck coordinates agents directly, inside the workspace.

Orchestrate

Launches and coordinates agents

Puck launches and coordinates agents for you across threads and execution environments.

Full context

Agent to agent handoffs

Trust

Coordination happens inside your workspace. No external processor receives digests of your sessions.

The trade 11

A third party now reads your session digests

Neither model is wrong. But the trust boundary is different, and the difference compounds.

Laminary's model

Summaries flow through an external service

Session digests go to Laminary's EU-hosted service. Their FAQ says no code and no chats unless shared, and notes are screened for injection. It is still a new trust boundary and a new MCP endpoint in every tool your team runs.

Amp's model

The shared record stays in your workspace

Access is workspace-scoped, group-scoped or audited. No external processor receives digests of your sessions, so the injection and leakage surface does not grow when coordination is added.

If a team is already inside one workspace, adding a connector is added risk for coordination it already has.

The market 12

Who actually needs Laminary?

The market is fragmentation, not coordination. Coordination becomes a product only when the tools are silos.

Needs it
  • Mixed-tool startups, 2 to 6 devs, where Cursor, Claude Code, ChatGPT and Codex all run at once.
  • Agencies and contractors handing work across different tools through copy-paste.
  • ChatGPT-only teams with no agentic tool and no shared project memory at all.
Doesn't need it
  • Amp teams: threads, Spaces, artifacts, multiplayer and Puck already provide shared memory, handoffs and clash avoidance.
  • Single-tool enterprise teams whose vendor ships shared context natively.
The bet: Laminary bets multi-tool fragmentation stays the norm. Amp bets teams converge on one agentic workspace with shared state. Your tooling mix decides which bet applies to you.
Verdict 13

Bottom line

  • Necessary with Amp? No. Every capability Laminary sells maps to an Amp-native feature, documented slide by slide above.
  • Useful as a bridge? Possibly, if your team really splits across Amp, Cursor, ChatGPT and Claude Code. That mixed-tool gap is the one thing Amp does not cover.
  • Cost of testing: free. The beta costs nothing, so the bridge case is cheap to try. For an Amp-only workflow, skip it: same features, no extra trust boundary.
